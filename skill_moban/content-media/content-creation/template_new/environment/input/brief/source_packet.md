# Source Packet

## One-line read

North America's latest power picture splits three ways: Canada is the clean-share outlier, Mexico remains the gas-heavy counterpoint, and the United States still moves at unmatched clean-power scale.

## Snapshot years

- Population series: 2024
- GDP series: 2024
- Annual CO2 series: 2024
- Electricity mix series: 2025

## Publishable facts

- C03_CANADA_CLEAN_SHARE: In 2025, clean sources supplied 77.0% of Canada's electricity mix, ahead of the United States at 43.0% and Mexico at 25.9%.
- C04_CANADA_HYDRO_LEAD: Hydropower led Canada's 2025 generation stack at 344.7 TWh, well ahead of gas and wind.
- C05_MEXICO_GAS_RELIANCE: Gas supplied 61.6% of Mexico's electricity mix in 2025, the highest gas share of the three markets.
- C06_MEXICO_LOWEST_CO2: In 2024, Mexico recorded 461.0 Mt of annual CO2 emissions, below Canada's 533.3 Mt and far below the United States at 4904.1 Mt.
- C07_US_CLEAN_SCALE: The United States generated 1,943.6 TWh from clean sources in 2025, the largest absolute clean-power total in this three-country set.
- C02_US_GDP_SCALE: In 2024, the United States posted a GDP of $28.75T, versus $2.24T for Canada and $1.86T for Mexico.
- C01_US_POPULATION_SCALE: In 2024, the United States had 340.1M people, compared with 130.9M in Mexico and 41.3M in Canada.

## Editorial angle

Lead with the clean-share gap first, then pivot to the gas-heavy Mexico line, and close the main comparison with the scale of U.S. clean generation. The GDP and population points are useful for background and newsletter framing.

## Watchouts

- Keep the share claims separate from the absolute-volume claims.
- Keep the latest-year labels attached to every key number.
- Use the emissions line as a contrast point, not as a full causal claim.
- Keep the closing CTA practical and light.
