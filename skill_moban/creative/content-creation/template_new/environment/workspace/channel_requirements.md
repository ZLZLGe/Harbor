# Channel requirements

## Shared rules

- Every channel must stay grounded in the launch brief and the frozen fact sheet.
- Keep one shared CTA URL across the full bundle.
- Use the primary keyword where it naturally belongs, but do not stuff it everywhere.
- Keep the language clear, specific, and publishable. Avoid hype words such as `revolutionary`, `game-changing`, `10x`, or `magic`.
- Each channel should feel native to its format. Do not copy the blog post verbatim into LinkedIn or the newsletter.

## Blog

- Markdown format with a single `#` title.
- Use the primary keyword in the title and early in the article.
- Keep the article long enough to feel publish-ready, with multiple `##` sections.
- Cover the approval queue, plan availability, language availability, and the 42% beta result.
- End with a clear waitlist CTA.

## LinkedIn

- Keep a concise opening hook on the first line.
- Use short paragraphs and a small number of hashtags.
- Mention the release name and keep the CTA aligned with the launch brief.
- Avoid sounding like a generic feature dump or a copy of the blog post.

## Newsletter

- `newsletter.json` must stay valid JSON.
- Include `subject`, `preview_text`, `body_markdown`, `cta_label`, and `cta_url`.
- The body should read like a short editorial update, not a landing page.
- Keep it grounded in the launch facts and the audience problem.

## SEO metadata

- `seo_meta.json` must stay valid JSON.
- Keep one primary keyword and a small list of secondary keywords.
- The title and description should align with the blog article and the launch brief.
- The slug should fit the launch topic and remain readable.
